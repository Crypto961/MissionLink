Why now

Three pressures arrive together

Each one alone would justify change. Together they make the current facility-based structure untenable.

Jan 1

The Gateway goes US-only

Our negotiators spend 50–70% of their time in it today. From January 1, only US-based staff may use it.

5×

Workload is uneven

Open claims per person range from 138 to 704 across facility teams. Effort, not geography, should set the load.

+19

We are growing fast

We go from 12 to 31 facilities in two years. The model must absorb them without adding a team for each one.

The structure

One owner per account, ten balanced teams, a US desk for the Gateway

Teams own payer portfolios, not facilities. Lebanon prepares every dispute and keeps every pricing decision; the US desk only enters the work into the Gateway.

Risks we are managing

Every major risk has an owner and a fallback

We misread the Gateway's onshore ruleHigh impact

Legal sign-off before the build; offshore access stays as a fallback until December 19.

The US desk falls behind as volume growsMedium

A backlog trigger adds staff within 2 weeks; bridge leads report it daily.

People read new levels as a demotionMedium

Provisional levels, protected pay, published criteria and one appeal each.

We lose IDR experts during the changeHigh impact

Retention bonus through January 2028 and an L4 path that does not require managing.

Model B's small desk or tools failModel B

Kill switches return us to Model A staffing; manual entry always stays available.
